Research Graph Team

The Research Graph Team project explored how researchers collaborate, by building an interoperable graph of research collaboration networks in Australia and abroad. It set out to show how researchers work together across disciplines and universities, and how those networks point to new collaborations, funding opportunities and ways to build research capability.

This page is a record of the completed project, and it has no further work planned.

What the project explored

A researcher weighing a new collaboration, such as work with a particular laboratory, first wants to know whether they already have a link to that team. The co-authorship network, and the close collaborators of each researcher, answer that question. The project made such networks open to exploration.

ORCID records formed the core of the graph. The project enriched them through the Research Graph Augment API with joint publications and joint research projects beyond the records held by the ORCID consortium.

It also took on the problem of entity resolution for research institutions, because the link between researchers and their institutions is the key to a useful collaboration network. Scholarly records and project descriptions often name an institution without a persistent identifier, so connecting outputs and researchers to their institutions is a difficult task of disambiguation, especially for names in several languages and for abbreviations.
